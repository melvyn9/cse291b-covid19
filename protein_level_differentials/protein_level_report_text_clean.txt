Protein-level differential abundance was evaluated to determine whether the peptide-level abundance differences observed for the selected model features reflected broader protein-level changes. For each model-selected peptide, all associated protein accessions were retrieved from the raw Variants TMT annotation table. Peptides mapping uniquely to one protein were used for the majority-rule assessment, while peptides mapping to multiple proteins were interpreted cautiously because their abundance signal cannot be confidently assigned to one protein.

For each uniquely mapped peptide associated with a candidate protein, Severe and Non-severe intensity distributions were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. A protein-level effect was assigned only if a majority of uniquely mapped, testable peptides from the same protein were significantly different between classes in the same direction. If this majority rule was not met, the observed signal was interpreted as peptide-level, variant-level, ambiguous, or unsupported at the protein level.
