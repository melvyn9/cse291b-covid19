Protein-level differential abundance was evaluated by examining whether the peptide-level abundance patterns observed for the model-selected variants were consistent across other peptides mapping to the same proteins. For each candidate protein, all peptide variants mapped to that protein were retrieved from the raw Variants TMT annotation table. Peptides that mapped uniquely to a single protein were used for the majority-rule assessment, while peptides mapping to multiple proteins were treated cautiously because their abundance signal cannot be assigned confidently to one protein.

For each uniquely mapped peptide, Severe and Non-severe intensity distributions were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Group medians, log2 fold changes, missing-value percentages, and direction of abundance change were computed. A protein-level effect was assigned only when a majority of uniquely mapped, testable peptides from that protein showed significant differential abundance in the same direction. If only the model-selected peptide was significant, or if significant peptides did not form a majority, the effect was interpreted as peptide-level or variant-level rather than protein-level.
